# Style System

Style is a primary constraint when explicitly provided. Combine it with the theme instead of replacing the theme.

## Style Strength and Color Distribution

Every generated image brief should make the style visible in at least three layers:

- character construction: stroke shape, structure, rhythm, silhouette
- material and edge treatment: crayon grain, paper edge, glass refraction, metal bevel, ink bleed
- color distribution: per-character colors, stroke gradients, outlines, shadows, sticker layers, accent motifs

For cheerful, cute, children's, festival, pop, sticker, toy, or handwritten directions:

- Avoid single-color lettering unless requested.
- Use 3-5 harmonious colors with clear assignment.
- Prefer per-character color changes, two-tone stroke fills, colored outlines, drop shadows, and sticker backing layers.
- Keep the background lighter and less saturated than the lettering.

For premium, logo, ink, or serious directions:

- Limited color is acceptable, but still define material contrast, outline, paper/foil/glass layer, or accent color.
- Monochrome should be intentional, not accidental.

## Handwritten / 手写风格

- Use for: warm, personal, children, lifestyle, literary, educational, friendly brands.
- Character rules: legible Chinese skeleton, natural stroke-width variation, slight irregular alignment, relaxed rhythm, extended dots or diagonals when useful.
- Materials: marker, crayon, colored pencil, watercolor, chalk, brush pen, oil pastel.
- Color distribution: assign different crayon/marker colors to different characters or stroke groups; add a light sticker outline or soft drop shadow; avoid one flat color for all characters unless requested.
- Composition: light background, sticker or notebook feel, poster title or cover title.
- Risks: becoming sloppy, pseudo-calligraphy, wrong characters. Emphasize exact text and legibility.

## Cute / 可爱风格

- Use for: children, pets, desserts, toys, light entertainment, family campaigns.
- Character rules: rounded corners, inflated forms, soft weight, low aggression, short friendly terminals.
- Materials: candy, jelly, foam, sticker, plush, paper.
- Colors: high brightness, pastel, controlled contrast; usually 3-5 colors across characters, outlines, and sticker layers.
- Risks: childish mismatch for adult products; decoration covering strokes.

## Ink / 水墨风格

- Use for: Chinese culture, poetry, tea, mountains, seasons, elegant festivals.
- Character rules: brush-informed but designed; dry/wet contrast, paper texture, clear structure.
- Materials: ink, rice paper, cinnabar seal, light wash.
- Composition: high negative space, restrained background, front-facing text.
- Risks: ordinary calligraphy instead of designed lettering; illegible cursive.

## Guochao / 国潮风格

- Use for: Chinese festivals, food, cultural products, youth campaigns.
- Character rules: bold Chinese structure, folk ornament, cloud/wave patterns, seal or poster influence.
- Materials: paper-cut, enamel, lacquer, gold foil, fabric.
- Colors: vermilion, jade green, warm gold, black ink, ivory; avoid using all at once.
- Risks: cheap red-gold clutter, unrelated dragon/phoenix stuffing.

## Cyberpunk / 赛博风格

- Use for: tech, games, nightlife, future city, music events.
- Character rules: compressed or modular Chinese structure, angular cuts, broken light bars, circuit-like joins.
- Materials: neon, black chrome, glass, LED, holographic edges.
- Colors: electric blue, magenta, violet, cyan, black.
- Risks: pseudo-Chinese symbols, unreadable fragmentation, dark background swallowing text.

## Metallic / 金属风格

- Use for: games, sports, action films, heavy product launches.
- Character rules: thick structural strokes, bevels, engraved cuts, sharp but readable edges.
- Materials: steel, iron, brushed metal, gold, black chrome.
- Composition: dramatic rim light, front-facing logo/title layout.
- Risks: overbeveled cheap 3D, too many sparks, damaged character skeleton.

## Glass / 玻璃风格

- Use for: beauty, tech, premium campaigns, fresh seasonal themes.
- Character rules: clean large shapes, rounded or geometric strokes, readable silhouette.
- Materials: translucent glass, jelly glass, frosted glass, refraction, glossy highlights.
- Colors: clear, cyan, soft pink, pearl, pale gold depending on theme.
- Risks: too transparent to read, cold mismatch for warm themes unless softened.

## Paper-Cut / 剪纸风格

- Use for: festivals, folk culture, children, handmade campaigns.
- Character rules: flat silhouette, connected cuts, decorative holes, clear outline.
- Materials: layered paper, red paper, colored card, shadowed cutout.
- Composition: centered title, clean background, limited layers.
- Risks: excessive tiny holes, losing character recognition, looking like generic stock festival art.

## Retro / 复古风格

- Use for: food, music, city memory, cafes, old posters, Hong Kong style.
- Character rules: strong title block, signboard rhythm, decorative but readable.
- Materials: print grain, enamel sign, aged paper, neon sign, old packaging.
- Colors: depends on era; avoid muddy uncontrolled palettes.
- Risks: nostalgia without character design; too many background props.

## Luxury / 高级奢华风格

- Use for: beauty, jewelry, wine, premium packaging, upscale brand titles.
- Character rules: refined structure, fewer decorations, controlled stroke contrast, elegant spacing.
- Materials: foil, embossing, silk, lacquer, pearl, marble, restrained glass.
- Colors: black, ivory, champagne, deep green, wine red, muted gold.
- Risks: "高级" ruined by clutter, too many colors, loud glow, stock ornament.

## Game Title / 游戏标题风格

- Use for: fantasy, sci-fi, martial arts, action, RPG, esports.
- Character rules: strong outer silhouette, heavy title presence, material tied to worldbuilding.
- Materials: metal, stone, fire, ice, jade, neon, bone-like forms depending on genre.
- Composition: centered logo-like title, high contrast, dramatic but readable.
- Risks: excessive texture; random weapons replacing strokes; unreadable fantasy shapes.

## Film Poster / 电影海报风格

- Use for: thriller, romance, action, drama, documentary, sci-fi.
- Character rules: cinematic spacing, mood-driven stroke treatment, high hierarchy.
- Materials: depends on genre: grain, shadow, light leaks, blood, smoke, neon, paper.
- Composition: title as primary subject or title integrated with poster mood.
- Risks: generating full movie poster when user only wants lettering; extra credits or English text.
